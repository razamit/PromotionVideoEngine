# Intake: ask what the request left open

Before making a slideshow or a short, find out what the person wants, so
they see the options instead of getting one default. Ask once, in one go,
and **only about what the request did not already say**. A request that
names the count, the format or style and the topic gets no questions.

Use the question tool when the session has one (up to four questions, each
with up to four choices plus the person's own answer). Without it, ask the
same questions as a short numbered list. Put the recommended choice first.

## How many comes first

"How many should I make?" is always the first open question. When the
answer is more than one, every other question offers **a mix** as its first
choice, so a batch of ten comes out varied instead of ten of the same.
When it is one, the first choice is "you choose" and you pick what fits
the topic.

## Slideshow questions

| # | Question | Choices |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How many slideshows? | 1 · 3 · 7 (one a day for a week) · 14 (a two-week test) |
| 2 | Which format? | A mix of all six · Lists and how-tos (`things-that-fixed`, `how-i`) · Stories (`problem-to-fix`, `before-after`) · Reveal and relatable (`reveal`, `relatable`) |
| 3 | Which look? | A mix · Cartoon (frames from the product's shorts and the house character; product slides stay real screenshots) · Real screenshots on a plain background · Text over photos you supply |
| 4 | What about? | A mix across the allowed claims · Free features only · Paid features only |

The person can always name one format or their own topics instead.
"Cartoon" needs a `cast` in the brief, and frames need shorts that can
still be rendered; if the product has neither, say so and leave that
choice out. "Photos" needs the photos; ask where they are.

A request for a slideshow **from a short** has its topic and its look
already (cartoon, from that short's frames), so only "how many" is asked:
how many slideshows from that short, one by default, up to three, each a
different format. The format question is replaced by the angle proposal in
slideshow-mode.md, "From a short", which is the one stop for approval. For
a batch across shorts ("a slideshow for each of these five shorts"), it is
one per short.

Not asked, because the product brief already decides them: aspect, file
format, baked or typed text, slide count. Say what they are in the plan.

## Video questions

| # | Question | Choices |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How many shorts? | 1 · 3 · 5 · 7 |
| 2 | Which style? | A mix of both · Bean (2D story, 45 to 60 s) · Toy (3D figures, 20 to 30 s) |
| 3 | How long? | Each style's own length · Short (15 to 20 s) · A mix of lengths |
| 4 | What about? | From the series backlog · New ideas · A mix of both |

Say which style the series has used so far (`.cartoon-shorts/app-series.md`,
"Built") next to the style question. A mix of styles on one account is
allowed when the person picks it; a single short never mixes them. Voice
and music are not asked: rotate through the roster and compose a bed per
episode, as the workflow already says, unless the request names them.

## Spreading a mix

- Deal the choices out in turn so each appears about equally often, and no
  two items in a row are alike where that can be avoided.
- With fewer items than choices, take the ones that fit the topics best.
- A mix of looks in a slideshow batch is per slideshow, not per slide: one
  deck keeps one look so it does not jump while swiping.
- Topics do not repeat inside a batch.

## Then one plan, one approval

For more than one item, show a single table and wait for a yes:

| # | Format or style | Look or length | Topic | Hook or logline |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Anything the brief or the series file decided goes under the table in one
line. The person can change any row. This table replaces the per-item
"pick the story" stop for a batch. For shorts, the script review (workflow
step 2) still happens for each short, unless the person says to run the
whole batch through.

For a single item, the usual stops apply: the slide table for a slideshow,
three loglines and then the script for a short.
